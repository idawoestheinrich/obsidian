The goal of the _CheapCal_ project is to create an affordable and easily constructed detector capable of identifying charged particles with a spatial resolution of approximately one centimeter.

The detector relies on an extruded plastic scintillator material characterized by a short light attenuation length. Both the front and back are structured with 16 parallel wavelength-shifting fibers, spaced uniformly at 1.5 cm, and aligned orthogonally. Silicon Photomultipliers (SiPMs) are utilized to read the light signals at each end of the fibers.

To ensure that a signal belongs to the current scattering event, the time resolution of the individual fibers must be determined. Using a Sr-90 beta source, the time response of each fiber is measured as a function of the beta-source position on the scintillator plate. For each fiber registering a signal, the mean arrival time value of both SiPMs is computed.

This measurement is also useful for evaluating the detector’s suitability for time-of-flight measurements.
