Timing resolution of a plastic-scintillator detector structured with wavelength-shifting fibres  and Quality control of wavelength-shifting optical modules for the SHiP Surrounding Background Tagger
## Abstract
This thesis addresses two independent topics, discussing the development and characterisation of two novel detector technologies based on organic scintillators read out via wavelength-shifting (WLS) light guides. 
The first part focuses on Wavelength-shifting Optical Modules (WOMs) designed to detect scintillation light in the Surrounding Background Tagger (SBT) of the SHiP (Search for Hidden Particles) experiment. Approximately 1600 WOMs are required for the SBT, and their performance must be consistent and verifiable. To this end, a dedicated test setup was designed and built, providing the basis for a future standardised quality control procedure based on responsivity and transmission measurements at different positions on the WOM surface. The assembly of the setup includes the construction of a dark box, a customised motion system, the integration of LED light sources, a photomultiplier tube (PMT), and photodiodes, as well as the development of a software interface for automatic measurements. During transmission measurements with a UV LED through the WOM wall, the destruction of the WLS dye (photobleaching of bis-MSB) was observed. An upper bound on the photobleaching rate of bis-MSB was experimentally determined, indicating that photobleaching during the SBT's operation is negligible. However, precautions are necessary during the production, storage, and quality control of the WOMs to prevent photodamage before deployment.
To mitigate UV-induced degradation of the dye, future improvements to the setup include replacing photodiodes with SiPMs and switching to a higher-wavelength LED source.

The second part of the thesis investigates the time resolution and response of a plastic scintillator detector prototype developed within the CheapCal project. The extruded plastic scintillator plate is structured with wavelength-shifting Kuraray Y-11 fibres read out on each end by Silicon Photomultipliers (SiPMs). The time resolution and signal arrival time were measured with a $\beta$-source in different positions on the detector plate. 
The detector shows position-dependent timing behaviour because some scintillation photons travel directly through the scintillator material to the SiPMs, bypassing the wavelength-shifting fibres. 
Hence, different combinations of fibres were analysed, resulting in a time resolution between $560\,\text{ps}$ and $810\,\text{ps}$.
