**Why do we want this domain?**

SHiP (Search for Hidden Particles) is an CERN-approved experiment designed to search for feebly interacting particles and study neutrinos using a dedicated beam-dump facility at the SPS. The collaboration currently has no dedicated public-facing domain and relies on an internal site not suited for external communication.

We are requesting **ship.cern** as the experiment's official public website, matching the naming convention already used by other CERN experiments (e.g. atlas.cern, cms.cern). We additionally request **hiddenparticles.cern** as an alias pointing to ship.cern. "Hidden particles" is not a generic term in this context — it is the direct expansion of the SHiP acronym and the core phrase used in our public communication ("Search for Hidden Particles"). The alias improves discoverability for non-specialist audiences who search using the experiment's subject matter rather than its acronym, while all content remains served from the single canonical ship.cern site.

**How do we intend to use it?**

ship.cern will be the collaboration's primary outward-facing website, targeted at policymakers, the CERN community, students and early-career researchers, science communicators, and the general public. Planned content includes:

- A "What is SHiP?" landing page explaining the physics case (dark matter, neutrino masses, matter-antimatter asymmetry) and experimental design (beam dump, shielding, detector concept)
- Pages for each subsystem, with visuals contributed by subsystem groups
- Collaboration and institute information
- A public FAQ with accessible physics explanations
- News on milestones, construction progress, and approvals (added once resources allow)
- A dedicated outreach contact address

The site will be built on CERN's centrally managed Wordpress hosting and will follow the CERN web design guidelines. It will link back to home.cern and include the required CERN footer elements. Development is coordinated by the SHiP Outreach group, with an appointed Outreach Coordinator and an approval flow involving the Physics and Technical groups to ensure accuracy. A first version is targeted for the next collaboration meeting (14th - 18th September 2026), to be expanded over time with a news section, test-beam gallery, and additional multimedia.
