### **Neutrino and Electron Disintegration in Particle Physics: Understanding Beta Decay**

In particle physics, **neutrino or electron disintegration** most commonly refers to processes where a nucleus or particle emits an electron (or positron) and a neutrino (or antineutrino), a phenomenon known as **beta decay**. This process is fundamental to understanding weak interactions and the conservation laws in nuclear physics.

#### Mechanism of Disintegration

- **Beta Decay (β-decay):** In beta decay, a neutron in a nucleus transforms into a proton, emitting an electron (β⁻ decay) and an antineutrino, or a proton transforms into a neutron, emitting a positron (β⁺ decay) and a neutrino. The emitted neutrino or antineutrino carries away missing energy and angular momentum, ensuring conservation laws are satisfied [2](https://consensus.app/papers/the-neutrino-concept-stern/00b6bed645ba512693e68578060f7fc1/)[3](https://consensus.app/papers/a-discussion-on-3type-of-n-uclear-t-ransm-utations-ellis/4e7978d311dc5453b0e7c3c4c77553b1/)[4](https://consensus.app/papers/the-%E2%80%9C-neutrino-%E2%80%9D-bethe-peierls/4d28862cd2605ab9beb0375b9bd4a472/).
- **Role of the Neutrino:** The concept of the neutrino was introduced to explain the continuous energy spectrum of emitted electrons in beta decay, which could not be accounted for by a two-body decay. The neutrino, being neutral and very weakly interacting, escapes detection but is essential for energy and momentum conservation [2](https://consensus.app/papers/the-neutrino-concept-stern/00b6bed645ba512693e68578060f7fc1/)[3](https://consensus.app/papers/a-discussion-on-3type-of-n-uclear-t-ransm-utations-ellis/4e7978d311dc5453b0e7c3c4c77553b1/)[4](https://consensus.app/papers/the-%E2%80%9C-neutrino-%E2%80%9D-bethe-peierls/4d28862cd2605ab9beb0375b9bd4a472/)[![Full text used](https://consensus.app/icons/file-check.svg)7](https://consensus.app/papers/neutral-electron-e%C2%B0-or-neutrino-%CE%BD-e%C2%B0-%E2%89%A1-%CE%BD-puccini/c8cbd5a41f3d56bba3de73346d4b1e6b/).
- **Double Beta Decay:** In rare cases, two neutrons decay simultaneously, emitting two electrons and two antineutrinos (or none, in neutrinoless double beta decay, if neutrinos are Majorana particles)

#### a) **Charged-Current Interaction**

- A neutrino exchanges a **W boson** with a nucleon, changing the nucleon’s type.
- Example: a neutrino interacts with a neutron inside a nucleus:
$$ν_e+n→p+e^−$$
Here:
- $\nu_e$ is an electron neutrino,
- $n$ is a neutron,
- $p$ is a proton,
- $e^−$ is an electron.
- 
- This can cause the **disintegration of the nucleus**, because changing a neutron to a proton can make the nucleus unstable.
#### b) **Neutral-Current Interaction**

- A neutrino exchanges a **Z boson**, scattering off a nucleon or nucleus $N$ without changing its type:
$$
ν+N→ν+N
$$
- The nucleus may absorb energy and break apart, but the neutrino type remains the same.
    
**Key idea:** Neutrino disintegration refers to the **breaking apart of a nucleus or nucleon due to neutrino interactions**, often producing other particles in the process
