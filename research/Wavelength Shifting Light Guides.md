# Wavelength Shifting Light Guides

Due to the finite attenuation length of the scintillator, its light collection efficiency and spatial resolution are usually limited by its size. That is why, in many cases, organic scintillators are equipped with [[WLS]] light guides. These light guides have an index of refraction similar to the scintillator and the glass of the photomultiplier attached. They also work on the principle of [[Total internal reflection|TIR]].

The shape of such a light guide can be chosen in a way that fits the form factors of the experiment and also favours the photon transport [[Kolanoski, 2016]].

This work discusses two different WLS light guides [[Wavelength shifting Fibers|WLS fibres]] and [[WOM]], both of which have a cylindrical shape. This shape has a huge advantage due to its circular base. ![[THEO_Light_Collection.png]]Once a photon is reflected on the inside of the circle with a radius $R$ and index of refraction $n_2$ in a different medium with an index of refraction $n_1$, it will continuously be reflected under the same angle $\alpha$ [[Hebecker, 2021]].

### Light  Guides
Due to these effects the light collection efficiency and spatial resolution of a scintillator is usually limited by its size, if the PM is connected to the Scintillator material directly.
That is why in many cases organic scintillators are equiped with wavelength shifting  "light guides".
These light guides have a index of refraction similar to the glass of the PA and also work on the principle of internal reflection. 
They can also be scintillators themselfs, but also other materials with a similar index of refraction, cladded or coated with a WLS dye.
