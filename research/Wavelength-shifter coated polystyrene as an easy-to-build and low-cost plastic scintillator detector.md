### In-text annotations
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">We studied the light yield of a pure polystyrene slide coated with wavelength-shifter  molecules, coupled to a photomultiplier, using β particles from a 90Sr source, as a possible  easy-to-build, low-cost plastic scintillator detector. Comparison measurements were performed with  an uncoated polystyrene slide as well as with uncoated and coated PMMA slides, the latter which  can only produce Cherenkov light when being traversed by charged particles. The results with the  single (double) coated polystyrene slides show about 4.9 (6.3) times higher detected photon yield  compared to the uncoated slide. For comparison, the light yield of a polystyrene-based extruded  plastic scintillator material doped with PTP and POPOP was measured as well. The absolute detected  light yield motivates future studies for developing easy-to-build, low-cost polystyrene-based plastic  scintillator detectors.</mark> [Page -](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=-&annotation=L7C4GJYM)
###### Introduction [Page 1](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=1&annotation=QTP2E8TD)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">They have been originally proposed  as water-Cherenkov photon detectors in the context of the Upgrades of the IceCube neutrino  telescope [2]. In fact, WOMs will be deployed for a seven-string extension of the IceCube detector  planned for the 2022/2023 South Pole deployment season [3]. Following the first proposal [2], they  have been also proposed as photon detectors for the liquid-scintillator-based Surround Background  Tagger of the SHiP experiment proposal at the CERN SPS [4, 5]</mark> [Page 1](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=1&annotation=I83RUZ9C)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">In this work, we modify the WOM concept in two ways:</mark> [Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=2&annotation=YIFF2QDV)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">) Instead of a tube geometry, we  consider a planar geometry, e.g. a slide. The WOM principle concerning the condition of total  reflection will be preserved in a planar geometry and therefore it is expected that many photons can be  guided to the thin-sized side walls of a slide.</mark> [Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=2&annotation=4SIW43Q5)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">The WOM base material serves now as the stand-alone  active detector material. Hence UV photons produced by Cherenkov radiation and, depending on the  material, possibly also by scintillation inside this material are wavelength-shifted and guided to the  photon detector.</mark> [Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=2&annotation=PZL2JLLL)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">For our planar WOM material, we consider here in particular  pure polystyrene (PS) that can be commercially purchased, at least for specific thicknesses varying  between 1 and 5 mm, at quite low cost</mark> [Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=2&annotation=XRRDQ8R9)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">Pure polystyrene is not only producing Cherenkov light but  is also scintillating, although with low light output, increasing the overall light yield.</mark> [Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=2&annotation=66AUTNA6)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">One can not expect light yields as  large as for standard PS-based plastic scintillators doped with a primary fluorophore and with WLS  molecules being mixed into the PS.</mark> [Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=2&annotation=LHVU9WYK)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">However, the advantage of the material and procedure studied  in our work is that one can produce without large efforts planar PS detectors by simply dip-coating  a commercially available, very cheap material that can be cut into the desired slide geometries.</mark> [Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=2&annotation=BQ3YWIC6)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">we studied with a β source various square-shaped PS slides of 50 mm side  length and about 5 mm thickness,</mark> [Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=2&annotation=K48I7EW8)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">s a comparison, also slides made of non-UV-transparent PMMA, which does not scintillate  and acts only as a Cherenkov light radiator when being traversed by a high-energy charged particle</mark> [Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=2&annotation=4MZIN8IG)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">For comparison, we studied as well an extruded scintillator material based on polystyrene with  a PTP concentration of 1.5% and a concentration of POPOP of 0.01% produced by the UNIPLAST</mark> [Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=2&annotation=Y9D84PSF)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">Such scintillators can be produced at moderate costs and  generate high light-yield, however with only moderate attenuation lengths of about 10 to 15 cm in  the visible range [11], which are even smaller than the attenuation length of pure polystyrene.</mark> [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=3&annotation=RYK95PG7)
###### 2 Experimental setup and measurements [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=3&annotation=ECZKPHAK)
###### 2.1 Sample preparation [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=3&annotation=X9FGRIDI)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">The dip-coating procedure was optimized in dedicated studies to maximize the absorption  probability of PMMA WOM tubes developed for the SHiP Surround Background Tagger</mark> [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=3&annotation=VJZCURFX)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">Coating with the standard paint results in WLS layers of about only 5 μm thickness, as  measured on dip-coated glass slides using the stylus profiler DektakXT from Bruker Corporation.</mark> [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=3&annotation=WT5J4L4K)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">Significantly higher thicknesses can be achieved using the modified paint with the maximal coating  speed of 150 mm/min achieved with the dip-coater in use (ND-DC Dip Coater from Nadetech  Innovations S.L.) and coating the slide twice.</mark> [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=3&annotation=ZDGAY6BT)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">With this procedure, WLS layer thicknesses on glass slides between  25 μm and 40 μm have been achieved with the modified WLS dye</mark> [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=3&annotation=PYRMYWF5)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">Determining the WLS layer thickness on a plastic material slide, either by comparing the weight  of the slide before and after dip-coating or by using the profiler method applied for coated glass  slides, does not work since the toluene dissolves a part of the plastic material surface during the  coating process.</mark> [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=3&annotation=WSXS9CNB)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">Comparisons of dip-coated glass slides with extruded and casted PMMA slides  however show that the transmission behaviour with respect to the WLS dye layer agree with each  other. Therefore, similar WLS layer thicknesses can be achieved on plastic material as on glass.</mark> [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=3&annotation=DUCK4VY5)

###### Results on the detected PE yield [Page 15](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=15&annotation=4H3UAR4N)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">Since the energy loss of a charged particle inside a thin layer of material follows a Landau distribution  and the non-negligible electronic noise follows a Gaussian distribution</mark> [Page 15](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=15&annotation=SIBSUJGP)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">To quantify the number of PE detected by the PMT,  we choose the most probable value (MPV) of this fit, translating it using the equivalence between PE  and mV ns obtained from the R1924A PMT calibration measurements as described above.</mark> [Page 15](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=15&annotation=Y4AJII78)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">The first quoted uncertainty  (σMPV) is the uncertainty of the weighted mean, quantifying the statistical uncertainty in the MPV  determination. The second quoted uncertainty (σsys) is calculated as the weighted standard deviation  of the two measurements from the MPVs mean, allowing us to estimate whether the decoupling  and coupling again the slide to the PMT introduces a significant systematic uncertainty. In addition,  there is about a 1% uncertainty for all measurements related to the SPE calibration value.</mark> [Page 15](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=15&annotation=PTVEQPBM)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">Let us first discuss the results for the PMMA slide. While PMMA does not scintillate, it can  result into radiation of Cherenkov light when being traversed by a charged particle with a velocity  above the speed-of-light inside PMMA, cPMMA = 1 nPMMA, with nPMMA = 1 520 at a wavelength  of 400 nm [15].</mark> [Page 17](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=17&annotation=KNSVB4CP)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">PMT depends on the incident  angle of the charged particles, since the Cherenkov radiation is emitted under a defined angle with  respect to the particle‘s flight direction.</mark> [Page 17](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=17&annotation=G6WSG5E6)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">The measurement for PMMA-WLS (PMMA-WLS-2)  shows a 1.4 (1.6) times higher PE yield compared to the one for the uncoated PMMA</mark> [Page 17](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=17&annotation=VC5M4BRA)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">A possible increase in light output  might be achieved for WLS-coated PMMA in case of UV-transparent PMMA plate material, which  however is typically significantly more expensive than UV-non-transparent PMMA plate material.</mark> [Page 17](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=17&annotation=2KBNBXZH)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">The measurement for PS-WLS (PS-WLS-2), shows  a 4.9 (6.3) times higher PE yield compared to the one for the uncoated PS slide as result of the  absorption of the scintillation as well as the Cherenkov UV photons in the WLS layer after a short  travelling distance and the subsequent re-emission of photons in the visible range profiting from  the high PS transparency in this wavelength range.</mark> [Page 17](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=17&annotation=9TDQB6PA)
- <mark class="hltr-yellow">In absolute terms, the detected light yield of  about 55 photoelectrons in case of the double-coated PS is sufficiently large to further study and  develop the concept as a detector technology, even if the light yield is a factor of 9.3 lower than for  the UNIPLAST extruded scintillator that was used for comparison</mark> [Page 17](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EK2JJ6E2?page=17&annotation=3CCBBJGM)
